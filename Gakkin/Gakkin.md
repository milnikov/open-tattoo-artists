## Bio
Gakkin, born Kenji Nishigaki in Wakayama, Japan, is a revolutionary tattoo artist renowned for his distinctive freehand blackwork technique that has transformed contemporary Japanese tattooing. Unlike most tattoo artists who rely on stencils, Gakkin draws directly on the body, allowing his designs to conform naturally to its curves and contours. His artistic journey began unexpectedly—after initially pursuing fashion design, he pivoted to tattooing in 1998 and developed his craft at Harizanmai Studio in Kyoto before relocating to Amsterdam, where he now operates his own studio. Gakkin's work is characterized by bold, flowing black designs often accented with strategic splashes of red, creating dramatic contrast and visual impact. His artistic influences span traditional Japanese motifs, natural elements, and ancient Japanese paintings, which he reinterprets through his unique contemporary lens. In his own words, "I believe that black is the most important color in tattooing. Every ancient tattooing culture – Maori, Japanese, and Polynesian – considers it as such. It just works better than any other color on the skin." His portfolio features large-scale pieces—full sleeves, back pieces, torso work, and complete bodysuits—that demonstrate his mastery of composition across the human form. Gakkin frequently collaborates with fellow Japanese blackwork artist Nissaco, creating seamless, harmonious designs that showcase both artists' complementary styles. With his meticulous attention to detail and distinctive artistic vision, Gakkin has earned international acclaim and a devoted following of over half a million Instagram followers, establishing him as one of the most influential and innovative tattoo artists in the world.
